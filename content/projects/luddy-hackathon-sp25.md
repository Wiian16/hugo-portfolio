In April of 2025, I competed in the third edition of the 
[Luddy Hackathon](https://luddy.indiana.edu/student-life/hackathon.html) along with my established team of Lillie
Donato, Sam Miller, and Matthew Widjaja. 

Our chosen case for this hackathon involved creating an AI-powered college course scheduling tool. We decided to name
our project iGPS++, a play on Indiana University's iGPS tool. Our product focused on creating a streamlined experience
with personalized course recommendations. 

Our final product contained the following features: 
- A fully featured GraphQL database for recursive data structures
- RESTful API endpoints for straightforward queries
- Streamlined front end built with React
- A personalized AI assistant for scheduling needs

My role, once again, was primarily a backend one. I focused on creating the data structures and the tools to access the
database. For this project, we used a GraphQL interface backed by a SQLite database that could be accessed from either
a REST API GraphQL endpoint or dedicated endpoints for frequently accessed data. 

This experience taught me important lessons about collaborative development and the unpredictable nature of software
development. There were many curve balls thrown at us, and with 48 hours to come up with a fix, we had to learn
quickly.
